# NASA CFT70 catalog verification

## Verified current record

The supplied NASA Life Sciences Portal URL is currently reachable and resolves to **UTMB Campaign 11**, with **Payload ID: CFT70**, Mission ID **UTMB Campaign 11**, location **UTMB FARU**, and record duration **3 years, 7 months, 4 days**. The page describes a 70-day, 6° head-down bed-rest campaign at the University of Texas Medical Branch Flight Analogs Research Unit. It lists 13 or 21 pre-bed-rest days by study group, 14 post-bed-rest days, 21 integrated experiments, and catalogued equipment images including MRI, DXA, isokinetic/muscle-function and exercise-device items. [1]

This is an archived campaign/mission record, not a personal monitoring stream. Its scientific context is a controlled spaceflight analog: the historical NASA NTRS record describes six integrated studies at FARU, including functional performance, integrated resistance/aerobic training, testosterone supplementation, appetite/satiety, AD ASTRA, and neurocognitive research. [2]

## Supported MuscleMap contribution

**Educational contribution:** a source-linked CFT70 context card or timeline can explain what a NASA bed-rest analog is, identify the historical campaign, and distinguish the named study domains and equipment from ordinary personal monitoring. The record supports education about study design and measurement context, not a health recommendation.

**Computed-data contribution:** only transparent, metadata-derived computation is supported by the verified record—for example, displaying the campaign identifier, historical phase labels/durations, experiment/equipment categories, and provenance links. The mission page does not expose participant-level measurements, acquisition parameters, calibration details, units, or a validated mapping from CFT70 observations to a person’s muscle status. Therefore it does **not** support a personal muscle score, individualized baseline, or clinical inference.

## Equipment, acquisition, and comparability limits

The record is based on screened healthy subjects in a facility-based, 6° head-down bed-rest protocol. Its equipment labels and images document the campaign context, but they are not equivalent to a consumer wearable or a user’s home/gym measurement setup. The campaign combines standard measures with investigator-specific protocols and multiple study populations/modalities; the catalog page does not establish that measurements across those protocols are interchangeable. [1] [2]

A CFT70 value or historical phase should not be treated as a directly comparable personal time series. The record provides no evidence that its equipment, acquisition timing, subject selection, bed-rest exposure, or protocol-specific outcomes match this prototype’s users. No scans, exercise plan, intervals, or other intervention is prescribed here.

## Access, approval, training, and redistribution caveats

The current NASA **Data Request** route does not expose a request form in the unauthenticated page response; it currently navigates to **“Sign In”** and shows **“Loading sign in / Preparing secure access.”** This is the current observed access challenge. No older access rule is substituted for it. [3]

The campaign description’s statement that subjects were “recruited, screened, and upon approval, placed in the bedrest facility” refers to participant enrollment, not approval for a MuscleMap user to access or redistribute archived data. [1] NASA’s current Guidelines & Tutorials page labels its training materials for Human Research Program principal investigators and teams and includes data-sharing and submission-plan resources; it does not, on the public page, state a general end-user training requirement for viewing this record. [4]

The historical NTRS presentation is marked **Distribution Limits: Public** and **Copyright: Public Use Permitted**. That statement applies to that presentation record. It is not evidence that underlying CFT70 participant-level datasets, derived files, or portal downloads may be redistributed. Until the signed-in NASA request workflow or the applicable dataset terms are available, treat redistribution and republication of underlying data as unverified. [2] [3]
