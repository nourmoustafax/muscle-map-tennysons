# Teleguided self-ultrasound evidence

**Scope and status.** This note verifies only the supplied DOI: *Teleguided self-ultrasound scanning for longitudinal monitoring of muscle mass during spaceflight* (Scott et al., iScience, 2021; DOI `10.1016/j.isci.2021.102344`). The DOI currently resolves to the Cell Press/iScience open-access article; the record and full text are also available through PubMed Central. [1] [2] [3]

## Supported contribution to MuscleMap

**Educational contribution.** The paper supports a narrowly scoped explanation that *teleguided self-ultrasound (Tele-SUS)* is a remote-guidance framework in which a non-expert operator acquires ultrasound images while an expert sonographer guides probe placement, motion, settings, and image-quality checks. In this study, astronauts acquired panoramic images of lower-extremity muscles with two-way voice and one-way video support. It is educational evidence for the concepts of remote guidance, panoramic acquisition, muscle cross-sectional area (CSA), and comparison with MRI—not a consumer self-scanning guide, diagnostic instruction, or exercise prescription. [2] [4]

**Computed-data contribution.** The supported derived quantity is muscle CSA from panoramic ultrasound images, obtained by manual tracing/averaging of analyzed slices and compared with MRI. In the study’s spaceflight cohort, panoramic ultrasound showed no significant difference from an expert sonographer’s post-flight images and no significant difference in change from MRI. The simpler single-point muscle-thickness approach did **not** validly detect change in this experiment. Thus MuscleMap may describe the paper as historical evidence for a research measurement/comparison concept, but this source does not establish a validated personal-monitoring algorithm, a general-population reference range, or a clinical decision rule. [1] [2] [4]

## Equipment, acquisition, and comparability limits

- The study used a GE Vivid q B-mode system with a 9-MHz, 50-mm linear-array probe, customized upper- and lower-leg guides, a dedicated ISS ultrasound system/rack, downlink, video, and remote expert support. These conditions are materially different from an unspecified home or consumer device. In-flight scans used reclaimed water as an acoustic medium in microgravity; that detail is not a generally transferable home-acquisition recommendation. [4]
- Panoramic imaging required a stable scanning plane, consistent probe translation, guide placement reproduced from the first session, light/minimal pressure to avoid muscle compression, and participant-specific pre-flight image references. Acquisition was repeated until quality was comparable or the communications/acquisition window ended. Of 492 attempted in-flight images, 449 (91%) were rated high quality; high-quality acquisition required multiple attempts and took 33–82 minutes in this study. These are study observations, not prescribed scan intervals or procedures. [2] [4]
- The evidence is not interchangeable with single-point thickness measurements: the authors reported discordance between MRI and ultrasound thickness change and concluded that thickness Tele-SUS was not valid for detecting muscle-size change. [2] [4]
- The panoramic validation was anchored to MRI and expert-sonographer acquisitions, with trained operators and remote guidance. The spaceflight sample was 11 astronauts (10 male, 1 female), and the authors explicitly call for larger external validation. Intra-rater acquisition and analysis variability was not assessed; self-positioning/time constraints limited the measured sites to anterior thigh and posterior calf. Therefore results should not be generalized to arbitrary devices, operators, body sites, populations, or personal longitudinal baselines. [2] [4]

## Approval, training, and redistribution caveats

The study states that it was approved by the NASA Johnson Space Center IRB, JAXA IRB, ESA Medical Board, and Human Research Multilateral Review Board, with written informed consent from all participants. Participants received about three hours of general ultrasound training plus two 60-minute Tele-SUS didactic sessions; the Tele-SUS team completed NASA Principles of Remote Guidance Training. This is evidence of a supervised research workflow, not permission to reproduce it clinically or at home. [4]

The article’s data/code statement says supporting datasets are held by NASA’s Life Sciences Data Archive and are available through a reasonable-request/approved process consistent with NASA IRB direction; MATLAB ultrasound-analysis code is available on request. Do not add or redistribute the study dataset or code based on this citation alone.
